Quick Start for Coordinators

Set up the shared lists and division destinations once. Each day, find bills or read a report, review the content and recipients, preview the output, then publish.

Setup & contacts

1. Sign in. Open Report Router in Outlook, expand Setup & contacts, choose your legislature, and sign in with your work account. Use Setup progress to jump to any step.
2. Connect the shared lists. Choose your legislative Team’s SharePoint site, or another site your reviewers can access. Create or connect the routing, audit and tracker lists. OneDrive cannot hold these lists.
3. Set division destinations. Add each division separately. Several divisions can use the same legislative channel. A notification tag can identify that division’s coordinator(s); it does not need to include everyone who receives email. Email-only routes are also supported.
4. Manage division email contacts. Quick-add a person or paste spreadsheet rows, then Preview and Apply. To edit the same contacts in Teams, use Set up the same contact list in Teams and add the existing list with Teams’ built-in Lists app. This does not synchronize Teams tag membership. Leave the optional matched-contact Teams mentions off if only coordinators should be notified.
5. Choose the daily report audience (optional). Use the division contacts, paste a distribution list, or combine them. Morning drafting creates an Outlook draft while the pane is running; it does not publish to Teams or send the draft.
6. Check the connection. Click Check my setup. Read warnings and errors before publishing. Optional live notification tests are separate. Finish setup closes the wizard; it does not certify that checks passed.

Daily workflow

1. Find or read. Use New bills to check the watch window and add selected bills, or select an Outlook report and click Read selected report.
2. Review. Check bill context, proposed divisions, recipients and deadlines. Exclude irrelevant items and correct suggestions. If you edit the source report in Outlook afterward, re-read the final report; Outlook draft edits are not automatically imported.
3. Preview. Continue to Preview, select delivery options, and inspect proposed output. Nothing is sent here.
4. Publish. Read the selected delivery summary. Confirm the reviewed distribution, then publish. Teams posts and division emails are sent; the daily report option creates a draft for separate review and sending from Outlook. Changing delivery choices or reviewed items clears confirmation.
5. Start review work. In Work queue, create a request with its required divisions, assigned roles and confirmed deadline. Publishing does not automatically create this request. The custom staff queue tab is separate from the built-in Lists contact tab and may need IT publication. Review workflow details.
Teach it as you go: when a bill lands with no division, type a keyword (like "CDL"), pick the division, and click Teach. The add-in remembers forever — next week that kind of bill routes itself.

Comment deadlines

Divisions get a response window — 48 business hours by default, ending at 5:00 PM. The Router does the calendar math for you: a Friday afternoon publish is due Tuesday at 5:00 PM, never over the weekend.

Set it in Setup: Response window (business hours) and Due time. Weekends and the six holidays every US public employer closes for (New Year's, Memorial, Independence, Labor, Thanksgiving, Christmas) are skipped automatically — including the federal rule that moves a Saturday holiday to Friday and a Sunday one to Monday. Nothing to maintain each year.
Under Which other holidays does your agency close for?, tick the ones you actually observe — MLK, Presidents, Juneteenth, Columbus, Veterans, day after Thanksgiving. These differ between agencies, so they're asked rather than assumed; the defaults are the federal list and a state agency's is usually shorter. Furlough days and local closures go in the other closures box.
The deadline appears in the Teams post's subject line (visible in the channel list before anyone opens it), in the post body, marked high importance (the red !), in the division emails, in the daily report, and as the Due date on every tracker row.

Costs and severity: the fiscal rollup

Divisions report what a bill would cost them. The Router collects those numbers and keeps a running total per fiscal year — the number you need at budget time.

How divisions report: whichever way they already do. They can fill in Estimated cost and Impact severity on their BillTracker row, or just reply in the Teams thread ("minimal impact, about $40,000 in FY27") or attach a spreadsheet.
You collect it: Budget & history tab → Harvest from Teams replies. The add-in reads the reply threads and any attached Word/Excel files, pulls out the dollar figures with the sentence they came from, and shows you a reviewable list. Edit anything that looks off, tick what's right, then Apply checked to tracker. Nothing is written until you apply it.
You report it: Load fiscal rollup shows totals per fiscal year — by division, by severity, and how many bills are still awaiting an estimate (a "TBD" is never counted as zero). Export CSV gives you detail rows plus a TOTAL line per year, ready to paste into a budget request.
Want charts instead? All of this lives in ordinary SharePoint lists, so Power BI can read it directly — outstanding-response scorecard, spend by fiscal year, average response time per division. The build recipe is in docs/powerbi; hand it to whoever runs Power BI at your agency.

Handing this to someone else

A backup coordinator, or whoever takes over: open Setup → Advanced → Hand this setup to someone else, type their email, and click Write the invitation. Review the draft and press Send. They open Report Router, click Find my setup, then Connect & load routing rules once — and they have your entire configuration. No code to copy, nothing to re-enter.

Tell your divisions what's expected

Send your reviewers the one-page guide for division reviewers — what the Teams post means, how to answer, and how to mark themselves done. Paste the link into your legislative channel description so it's always there.

Things to know

  • Nothing ever sends by itself. Teams posts and emails happen only when you click Publish and confirm; the daily report always waits in your Drafts for you to send.
  • If you run the same report twice, nothing duplicates. The add-in remembers what it already posted.
  • Your setup is saved to your account. New computer, new email, same setup — nothing to redo.
